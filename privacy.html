Consent — with him, not about him

not recorded
Singapore's Mental Capacity Act 2008 starts from a presumption: a person is assumed to have capacity unless it is shown otherwise. It also says you must not treat someone as lacking capacity “without first doing what can practicably be done to support them in making the decision” — and that an unwise decision is not evidence of incapacity. At mild stage he very often still has capacity. So we ask him.
1

Explain it to him, in his own language

Read aloud through the glasses, in the language he thinks in, slowed to his speech profile. This is the “practicable support” the Act asks for — it is the difference between consent and paperwork.

Ask at a good time of day. Not late afternoon, not when he is tired.

What he agreed to

Granular, because the PDPA's Purpose Limitation obligation means consent for one purpose is not consent for another. He can say yes to being helped and no to being measured.

Co-signed by the donee

Under an LPA the donor grants authority over personal welfare, property & affairs, or both. A property donee cannot make healthcare decisions unless also authorised for personal welfare — so we record which scope you actually hold.

No LPA? He can still make one while he has capacity, and it is far easier now than later. DementiaHub.SG and the Office of the Public Guardian explain how.

ongoing assent
Taking the glasses off is a withdrawal, not a fault. If wear time falls away, the system tells you and stops — it never nags him back into it. Consent that cannot be withdrawn in practice was never consent.

His rights, as buttons

The PDPA gives a right of access and correction, and from 2022 a right to data portability. Rights that require an email and a 30-day wait are rights on paper.

Five decisions, not five promises

If something goes wrong

A breach is notifiable if it is likely to cause significant harm to an affected individual, or affects 500 or more individuals. We must tell the PDPC within 72 hours of determining it is notifiable — the clock runs from that determination, not from discovery — and tell affected individuals where significant harm is likely.

Why our blast radius is small.

There is no video. There are no images of anyone who did not consent. A stolen descriptor is 128 floating-point numbers that cannot be turned back into a face. The worst realistic breach exposes a list of about twenty names one family chose to enrol — which is bad, and is architecturally as small as we could make it.
